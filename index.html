0–4%

Models can detect impossibility but usually do not admit it! When we ask "Is it still solvable?", models get it right up to 100% of the time. When we ask for the best next move to win in the same state, all models except GPT-5.4 report it only 0 to 4% of the time.

77%

Models override their own reasoning. In up to 77% of wrong answers, the reasoning trace already says the puzzle is unsolvable, but the model still gives a move.

80–100%

Models blame more recent actions. When asked which action caused the failure, 80 to 100% of wrong answers point to a later step than the real cause.

We curate out-of-distribution puzzle environments where every move carries an irreversible effect. Puzzles work well here because simple solvers give us the exact right answer for every step, and new game mechanics stop models from relying on memorized solutions. We also check every puzzle against greedy shortcut strategies, so that solving it needs real planning.

01

The integration gap

We test the same state through two different prompts: "Is it still solvable?" vs "What is the best next move to win?". Except GPT-5.4, most models do well when asked explicitly but fail badly when asked for the next move. We tested each model on 300 unsolvable puzzles, with 100 from each game.

02

Self-override in model responses

Through model traces on questions where they gave the wrong answer, we discovered that models realise that a given state is unsolvable, but choose to give a move anyway, overriding their findings. They spend a lot of tokens in this dilemma: wrong answers from Qwen3.5-397B average 37,581 tokens.

■ knows it can't win■ gives a move anyway
03

Tracing failure to its cause

Given the initial state of the puzzle and a list of actions, we ask models to identify the action that first made the puzzle unsolvable. When they get it wrong, their answer usually lands on the step where the failure becomes obvious, not the action that actually caused it. Most models also get worse as the gap between the cause and the end of the trace grows.
